# Thinking Framework Extraction Method

> The core method for turning raw material into a mental model you can run.

## 1. The Triple Test for Mental Models

To count as a "mental model", a claim must pass the triple test. A claim that fails is just a passing remark.

### Test 1: Cross-domain recurrence
The same thinking framework appears in at least 2 different domains the person talks about.

Example: Munger's idea of "incentives".
- He uses incentives to explain business results (how a sales team is paid predicts its behavior).
- He uses incentives to explain human misjudgment (people believe what pays them to believe).
- He uses incentives to explain institutions (bad rewards produce bad organizations).

It recurs across 3 domains. This is a real mental model.

### Test 2: Generative power
You can use the model to predict the person's stance on a new question.

Example: if Munger's "inversion" is a mental model:
- Asked "how do I succeed?", he first asks "how do I make sure I fail?"
- Asked "how do I invest?", he first asks "how do I lose all my money?"

It generates new predictions. This is a real mental model.

### Test 3: Distinctiveness
Not every smart person thinks this way. The model shows the person's unique point of view.

Example: "antifragility" belongs to Taleb. Not everyone sees the world this way.

It sets the person apart. This mental model is worth extracting.

**If a claim passes only 1 test**, downgrade it to a "decision heuristic", not a "mental model".
**If a claim passes 0 tests**, it is probably something the person said in one specific context. Leave it out.

## 2. Measuring Expression DNA

### 2.1 Sentence fingerprint

Pick 20 random paragraphs from the person's long-form writing or talks. Measure the following:

| Dimension | How to measure |
|------|---------|
| Average sentence length | Words / sentences |
| Question ratio | Questions / total sentences |
| Analogy density | Analogies per 1,000 words |
| First-person rate | How often "I" appears |
| Certainty ratio | "definitely", "obviously" vs "maybe", "perhaps" |
| Pivot frequency | "but", "however", "yet" per 1,000 words |

### 2.2 Style tags

Tag the person on each of these scales:

```
Formal       <-> Conversational
Abstract     <-> Concrete
Cautious     <-> Assertive
Academic     <-> Plain-spoken
Long sentences <-> Short sentences
Builds up to the point <-> Leads with the conclusion
Data-driven  <-> Story-driven
```

### 2.3 Banned words and verbal tics

- Words the person never uses: the skill must not use them either.
- The person's verbal tics and favorite phrases: use them in moderation. Too many turns the skill into an impression act.

## 3. Handling Contradictions

Contradictions are a core trait of a personality. They are not bugs to fix.

### Three types of contradiction

1. **Contradictions over time** (views that evolved)
   - The person said A early on and B later.
   - What to do: record how the view evolved. Label each view "early" or "recent".
   - In the skill, lead with the recent view, but mention the evolution.

2. **Contradictions across domains** (different rules for different settings)
   - The person argues for X at work and Y in private life.
   - What to do: record each domain on its own. Do not force them into one rule.
   - This is exactly where depth comes from.

3. **Internal tensions** (values that pull against each other)
   - Example: they chase freedom and also prize discipline.
   - What to do: record this clearly as a "core tension".
   - This is usually the most interesting part of the person.

### Wrong ways to handle them
- ❌ Pick one side and ignore the other.
- ❌ Invent an explanation that reconciles them.
- ❌ Pretend the contradiction does not exist.

## 4. When Information Is Thin

| Situation | What to do |
|------|------|
| Little public information on a dimension | Mark it in the skill: "Not enough information. This dimension is inferred." |
| Only secondary sources exist | Lower the confidence. Mark it "According to [source]". |
| Sources conflict and you cannot decide | Show both side by side. Let the user judge. |
| The person deliberately keeps something private | Respect the boundary. Note in the skill: "This person stays silent on this topic." |

---

## 5. Person Skills vs Topic Skills

| Dimension | Person skill | Topic skill |
|------|----------|----------|
| Core | How one person thinks | A thinking toolkit for one field |
| Source of mental models | Mainly one person | A mix of many viewpoints |
| Voice | Mimics the person's voice | Neutral but expert |
| Contradictions | Keep the person's internal tensions | Show the disagreements between schools |
| How to check | Compare against the person's known stances | Compare against the field's consensus |

## 6. Quality Self-Check

After you generate a skill, check it against these questions.

### Mental models
- [ ] Does each model have evidence from at least 2 different domains?
- [ ] Are there between 3 and 7 models? (Too few means too shallow. Too many means you did not distill.)
- [ ] Does each model state where it applies and where it breaks down?
- [ ] Do the models create tension with each other without contradicting each other?

### Expression DNA
- [ ] Is the voice recognizable, and not like a generic AI?
- [ ] Does it avoid overdoing the imitation until it becomes a caricature?
- [ ] Does it capture the core traits and not just surface mimicry?

### Decision heuristics
- [ ] Is each rule backed by a concrete case?
- [ ] Can a new situation trigger it (not only the original case)?

### Honest boundaries
- [ ] Does it state clearly what it cannot do?
- [ ] Does it list the sources and the research date?
- [ ] Does it admit which dimensions lack information?

### Overall
- [ ] If you look at a new problem through this person's eyes, do you get a useful point of view?
- [ ] Does it run the framework, and not just stitch together the person's quotes?
- [ ] If you remove the name, can you still tell whose thinking this is?
